AC Milan's 3–1 victory over Inter was shaped by a decisive first half. Milan did not rely on one attacking route: they created danger through a wide delivery, a corner and a direct free-kick, with Diego Moreira involved in all three goals.

## Milan attacked with variety

Milan struck first in the fourth minute. Moreira found space down the flank and delivered for Christian Pulisic, who headed home. Six minutes later, Moreira supplied another dangerous delivery, this time from a corner, and Adrien Rabiot doubled Milan's lead.

The two goals came from different situations, but both showed how Milan could turn wide-area involvement into immediate penalty-area danger.

Inter responded through Alessandro Bastoni, who scored from a corner. The visitors created further opportunities, but Mike Maignan produced several important saves to keep Milan ahead.

## Moreira's decisive contribution

Shortly before half-time, Moreira scored directly from a free-kick to restore Milan's two-goal advantage. His influence was central to the first-half outcome: he created the first two goals and finished the third.

The second half was more subdued. Inter tried to find a route back into the match, but Milan remained organised and protected their advantage.

## Key Tactical Findings

- Milan's wide delivery created an early attacking advantage.
- A corner provided the second goal.
- Inter also threatened from set pieces but could not sustain a comeback.
- Moreira's delivery and direct free-kick were decisive.

## TGO Analyst View

Milan's advantage came from turning different delivery situations into goals. The variety mattered: Inter had to defend open-play service, corners and a direct free-kick, while Milan's early lead allowed them to manage the second half with greater control.

## Conclusion

Milan won the derby by being more decisive in the moments that mattered. Their early wide threat and set-piece execution created the advantage, while their defensive work after half-time protected it.
