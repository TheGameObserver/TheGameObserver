## Executive Summary

Lionel Messi's final appearance for Argentina showed a different form of control.

He did not need to dominate every possession or remain permanently central to influence the match. Starting from the right of Argentina's 4-3-3, Messi moved across the attacking line and became most influential when Argentina reached decisive areas.

His corner created Nicolás Otamendi's opener, his pass released Nico Paz for the second, and he converted the penalty that completed the victory.

The pattern was clear: Messi's influence was concentrated in the moments where possession became opportunity.

## Match Context

Argentina defeated Benin 3–0 at the Monumental on 6 October 2026, giving Messi a winning farewell in his final appearance for the national team.

The first half ended goalless, with Benin resisting Argentina's attacking pressure.

After the interval, Argentina finally broke through — and Messi was directly involved in everything that followed.

## Tactical Setup

Argentina began in a 4-3-3, with Messi starting from the right side alongside Lautaro Martínez and Julián Álvarez.

The position provided Argentina with width while allowing Messi the freedom to move toward central and inside-right areas.

Behind him, Argentina's midfield could circulate possession without requiring Messi to become the team's primary progression mechanism on every attack.

That distinction became important.

Messi's role was less about touching the ball constantly and more about being available when an attack reached a position where his technical quality could change the outcome.

## Key Tactical Battle

Argentina controlled the territory, but control alone did not immediately break Benin's resistance.

Benin's compact defensive organisation made the first half relatively difficult to convert into clear attacking outcomes.

Messi therefore became increasingly important as Argentina looked for different ways to create the breakthrough.

The first came from a set piece.

Messi's corner found Nicolás Otamendi, who opened the scoring after the interval.

The second came through open play.

Messi then found Nico Paz, who produced the finish to double Argentina's advantage.

Finally, Messi converted a penalty to complete the scoring.

Three goals. Three different attacking actions.

That variety is more significant than simply recording three goal contributions.

## Messi's Influence

The three goals revealed three versions of Messi.

**The creator from a set piece.**  
His delivery gave Argentina a direct route through a fixed defensive structure.

**The creator in open play.**  
His pass to Paz showed his ability to recognise the final action without needing to carry the entire attack himself.

**The finisher.**  
When the opportunity came from the penalty spot, Messi assumed direct scoring responsibility.

This was selective influence rather than constant control.

Argentina's structure gave Messi the freedom to choose when to become the decisive player, while the movement around him created enough variety for his positioning to remain difficult to predict.

## The Wider Tactical Lesson

The first half demonstrated that territorial superiority does not automatically produce goals.

Argentina needed better execution in the final third, not simply more control of the match.

Messi's second-half influence showed how that problem could be solved without fundamentally reorganising the team.

The breakthrough came through his delivery.  
The second goal came through his final pass.  
The third came through his finishing.

Argentina did not need Messi to control every phase.

It needed him to control the moments that mattered most.

## Conclusion

Messi's final Argentina performance was defined by **selective influence**.

From a nominal right-sided position, he remained connected to Argentina's attacking structure while retaining the freedom to move toward the areas where he could make the greatest difference.

His final international appearance therefore offered a useful illustration of how his role has evolved.

Messi no longer needs to dominate every phase to dominate a match's decisive moments.

Against Benin, Argentina created the platform. Messi supplied the execution.

And in his final night in the Argentina shirt, that was enough to decide the game.

## Key Takeaways

- Messi started from the right of Argentina's 4-3-3 but operated as a broader creative reference.
- His three goal contributions came through three different mechanisms: corner delivery, open-play assist and penalty finishing.
- His influence was concentrated in high-value attacking moments rather than requiring constant involvement in every phase.
- Argentina's structure created the platform for Messi's technical quality to become decisive.
