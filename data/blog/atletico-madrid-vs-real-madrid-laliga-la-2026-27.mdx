## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Atlético Madrid beat Real Madrid 2–1 at the Riyadh Air Metropolitano after a goalless first half. The red card shown to Dean Huijsen became the decisive event, but Atlético's control of the match had already begun before it.

Simeone's 4-4-2 kept the centre compact and made it difficult for Madrid to connect their double pivot with Arda Güler, Jude Bellingham, Vinícius Júnior and Kylian Mbappé. Madrid had few clean central connections and the opening half remained cautious.

Simeone then introduced Koke for Marc Pubill at half-time, moving Marcos Llorente toward the defensive line and adding another passing reference in midfield. Atlético started the second half with greater attacking intent.

Huijsen was sent off after bringing down Giuliano Simeone in the area. Alejandro Grimaldo converted the resulting penalty, and six minutes later Giuliano supplied Jonathan David for Atlético's second.

Madrid changed personnel and found a late goal through Antonio Rüdiger, but Atlético had already turned their 11v11 defensive control into a numerical and territorial advantage.

## MATCH CONTEXT

The Madrid derby was Matchday 7 of the 2026/27 La Liga season and took place at the Riyadh Air Metropolitano on 20 September 2026.

Atlético started in a 4-4-2 with Giuliano Simeone and Jonathan David leading the line, while Real Madrid used a 4-2-3-1 with Valverde and Tchouaméni behind Güler, Bellingham and Vinícius, and Mbappé as the central reference.

The first half was physical and cautious. Both sides protected the central areas and neither established sustained attacking control. Contemporary reporting recorded only one first-half shot on target, reinforcing the impression of a match in which defensive organisation was limiting attacking connections.

## TACTICAL SETUP

### Atlético's 4-4-2

Atlético's shape created two compact defensive lines.

Jonathan David and Lee Kang-in could screen the central build-up, while the midfield four stayed close enough to protect the space in front of the defence. The structure did not require constant high pressing. Its main value was the short distance between the lines.

That made central progression difficult for Madrid. Valverde and Tchouaméni could receive, but finding Güler, Bellingham or Mbappé in useful positions was less straightforward.

Atlético also looked to the right side for attacking transitions, where Giuliano could combine with Llorente and Lee to attack the space around Marc Cucurella.

### Real Madrid's 4-2-3-1

Madrid's 4-2-3-1 offered several attacking players between Atlético's midfield and defence.

The problem was not the nominal structure. It was the difficulty of connecting the structure.

Atlético's compactness reduced the time available for Madrid's attacking midfielders to receive and turn. Vinícius and Mbappé were therefore less able to accelerate into the spaces they normally need.

The first half became a battle over access rather than possession: could Madrid connect the double pivot to the attacking four before Atlético's midfield line closed the route?

For much of the half, Atlético won that battle.

## KEY TACTICAL BATTLE

### Atlético protected the centre

Atlético did not need to press Madrid constantly to make the 4-4-2 effective.

Their priority was keeping the midfield line close to the forwards and denying clean central progression. Madrid were often forced to circulate around the block rather than through it.

This also explains why the match remained low-event before half-time. Atlético were not simply defending deep; they were reducing the quality of the connections Madrid needed to turn possession into attacks.

### Giuliano provided the escape route

Giuliano Simeone gave Atlético a different attacking option.

From the right, he could drop into the midfield line and then attack the space outside Madrid's centre-backs. That movement became decisive after half-time.

Hancko's forward pass released Giuliano behind Madrid's defensive line. Huijsen brought him down inside the area, producing a penalty and, after VAR review, a red card.

The action was individual, but it also reflected a recurring Atlético mechanism: defend compactly, recover the ball and attack the space behind the opponent's defensive line.

### Koke changed the rhythm

Simeone introduced Koke for Pubill at half-time. Llorente moved toward right-back while Koke added an additional midfield passing and circulation reference.

The adjustment mattered because Atlético did not simply need to defend their shape. They needed to become more comfortable with the ball once the second half opened up.

Koke helped provide that control.

## THE TURNING POINT

Huijsen's dismissal changed the match from a close 11v11 tactical contest into an 11v10 game.

Grimaldo converted the penalty to make it 1–0.

Six minutes later, Giuliano again attacked the right side and delivered the ball for Jonathan David to score. The sequence showed how quickly Atlético could convert numerical superiority into attacking advantage.

The important distinction is that the red card did not create Atlético's control from nothing. It amplified a control that had already been visible in the first half and strengthened by Koke's introduction.

## MADRID'S RESPONSE

Mourinho reacted to the dismissal by replacing Güler and Vinícius with Antonio Rüdiger and Yan Diomande.

Madrid's ten-man structure became more direct, with Diomande providing a route for individual attacking actions and Rüdiger adding defensive and set-piece presence.

That eventually produced a goal. Bernardo Silva delivered a free-kick and Rüdiger headed Madrid back into the match in the 89th minute.

The late goal created pressure, but it did not represent a restoration of Madrid's earlier attacking structure. Atlético had already spent most of the second half controlling where the game was being played.

## TACTICAL EVIDENCE

**1. The 11v11 structure already favoured Atlético's defensive objective**

The first half ended 0–0 and was characterised by limited attacking access. Atlético's compact 4-4-2 reduced the space between their midfield and defence and made central connections difficult.

**2. Koke gave Atlético another midfield reference**

His half-time introduction allowed Llorente to move to right-back while Koke strengthened the midfield circulation. This was a structural adjustment before the decisive red card, not merely a reaction to it.

**3. Giuliano connected transition and final-third attack**

His run produced the penalty and Huijsen's dismissal. His later delivery created Jonathan David's goal. Those two actions came from different phases but depended on the same quality: aggressive movement into space.

**4. The red card changed the game-state**

Once Madrid went down to ten, Atlético could attack against a reduced defensive structure while Madrid also had to chase the match. The resulting numerical advantage allowed Atlético to sustain pressure rather than continually breaking down an organised eleven-man block.

**5. Madrid's late response was specific rather than sustained**

Rüdiger's 89th-minute header gave Madrid a route back into the match, but Atlético remained capable of defending the lead. The late goal therefore changed the scoreline without restoring the overall tactical balance.

## IMPORTANT ADJUSTMENTS

**Initial situation:** Atlético's compact 4-4-2 restricted Madrid's central attacking connections.

**Adjustment:** Koke replaced Pubill at half-time, with Llorente moving to right-back.

**Decisive event:** Huijsen was sent off after bringing down Giuliano in the area.

**Atlético's response:** Grimaldo converted the penalty and Giuliano soon created Jonathan David's second goal.

**Madrid's response:** Rüdiger and Diomande replaced Güler and Vinícius as Mourinho reshaped his ten-man side.

**Final effect:** Atlético used the extra player to control territory and protect the lead, while Madrid's late set-piece goal created a final period of pressure.

## KEY PLAYER: GIULIANO SIMEONE

Giuliano was central to both decisive attacking moments.

His run behind the defensive line created the situation that led to Huijsen's red card. Later, he supplied Jonathan David for the second goal.

The importance of his performance was therefore not simply in producing two key actions. He gave Atlético a direct attacking outlet against Madrid's defensive line and then remained useful once Atlético had established territorial control.

## WHAT COULD HAVE CHANGED?

After Huijsen's dismissal, Madrid's priority had to change.

Rather than attempting to reproduce their original 4-2-3-1 attacking structure, they needed to protect the central penalty-area zones, limit Atlético's ability to move the ten-man block from side to side and preserve a route for counterattacks.

That is an analytical alternative rather than a claim about what would definitely have changed the result.

The key problem was that Atlético were able to turn their numerical advantage into repeated territorial pressure. Madrid eventually found a goal, but only after spending much of the second half without sustained attacking control.

## WIDER TACTICAL IMPLICATION

The derby showed two different versions of Atlético in the same match.

Before the red card, Simeone's side used compactness to restrict Madrid's attacking connections.

After the red card, they used Koke's circulation and the extra player to control the game with the ball.

That progression matters. Atlético did not need to abandon their defensive identity to become more dominant. The same compact structure that limited Madrid's attack gave them a platform from which to exploit the game once the numerical balance changed.

For Madrid, the match exposed the difficulty of maintaining attacking connections against a compact 4-4-2 and then reorganising quickly after losing a defender.

## CONCLUSION

Atlético's 2–1 victory was decided by Huijsen's red card, but the tactical story began before the dismissal.

Their 4-4-2 had already reduced Madrid's central access and made it difficult for Güler, Bellingham, Vinícius and Mbappé to connect consistently.

Koke's half-time introduction improved Atlético's circulation and gave the team another way to control the rhythm.

The red card then transformed that control into numerical superiority.

Giuliano Simeone connected the two phases: his movement produced the penalty and his delivery created the second goal. Madrid's late Rüdiger header made the ending uncomfortable, but it did not restore sustained control.

Atlético therefore won through a sequence rather than a single incident:

**compact defending → midfield adjustment → attacking transition → numerical advantage → game-state control.**

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. Atlético's compact 4-4-2 restricted Madrid's central attacking connections before half-time.
2. Koke's introduction strengthened Atlético's midfield circulation before the decisive incident.
3. Giuliano Simeone provided the key transition threat and created both decisive attacking sequences.
4. Huijsen's red card transformed an even 11v11 contest into an 11v10 tactical problem.
5. Atlético used the numerical advantage to control territory and manage the game.
6. Madrid's late set-piece goal created tension without restoring sustained attacking control.

## TGO ANALYST VIEW

Atlético's performance illustrates the difference between **creating control and exploiting control**. Their compact structure first reduced Madrid's attacking access. The half-time adjustment then improved their ability to play through the match, while the red card gave them the numerical advantage to turn that control into decisive territory.
