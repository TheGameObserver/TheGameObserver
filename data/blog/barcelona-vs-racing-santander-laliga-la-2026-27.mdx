# PLAYER DETAILS

Raphinha’s nominal position was on Barcelona’s left, but his attacking function frequently moved into central areas. Rather than remaining fixed as a conventional winger, he operated closer to the centre-forward zone and connected Barcelona’s midfield combinations with its attacking line.

That movement became one of the defining features of Barcelona’s attacking structure against Racing Santander.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Barcelona’s 7–2 victory over Racing Santander showed how a player can retain a nominal position while performing a very different tactical role.

Raphinha repeatedly moved inside from the left, occupying central attacking spaces and combining with Dani Olmo while allowing other players to provide width. His movement gave Barcelona another player between the lines and another presence around the penalty area.

The important tactical point was not simply that Raphinha scored a hat-trick. It was that his movement changed the relationships around him. Barcelona could attack with Raphinha centrally, Olmo connecting play, and wide players such as Lamine Yamal and Karim Adeyemi stretching the opposition.

# MATCH CONTEXT

Barcelona defeated Racing Santander 7–2 at Spotify Camp Nou on 16 September 2026 in Matchday 6 of La Liga.

Raphinha scored three times. His first goal came from the penalty spot after he was fouled for the opening penalty. He then scored from open play after a combination involving Dani Olmo before converting another penalty later in the match.

The match also provided a clear example of Raphinha’s central movement. Contemporary reporting described him as thriving in a centre-forward role, while Barcelona’s attacking sequences repeatedly placed him in areas normally associated with the central forward.

# TACTICAL SETUP

Barcelona started from a 4-3-3.

Raphinha was nominally positioned on the left, but his positioning was not consistently that of a traditional touchline winger. He frequently moved inside and operated closer to the centre-forward channel.

This created a different set of relationships.

Dani Olmo could connect play between midfield and attack while Raphinha occupied a more advanced central position. Yamal and Adeyemi could provide width and attack spaces around the defensive line.

The result was a front line that could change its reference points without Barcelona needing to abandon the basic 4-3-3 structure.

Racing Santander used a 4-1-3-2. That structure gave them a central defensive reference but also created a tactical question when Raphinha moved away from the left: should the nearest defender follow him centrally, or should Racing preserve the shape and allow him to receive?

Barcelona repeatedly benefited from that uncertainty.

# KEY TACTICAL BATTLE

## Raphinha’s movement created an extra attacking connection

### Problem

Raphinha starting from the left could have left Barcelona with a conventional winger-forward relationship.

Instead, his movement into central areas created another problem for Racing’s defensive structure.

The more Raphinha moved inside, the less clearly he could be treated as a fixed wide player. He could receive closer to the centre-forward position, combine with Olmo and attack the penalty area without having to begin each action from the touchline.

### Structure

Barcelona retained width through other players.

That was important because Raphinha’s central movement did not simply make the attack narrower. Yamal and Adeyemi could occupy wider positions while Raphinha moved into the spaces created inside.

Olmo’s positioning added another connection between the midfield and attacking line.

The structure therefore allowed Barcelona to have both central presence and width.

### Movement and Behaviour

Raphinha’s movement was most valuable when it happened before the final action.

He could drop toward the ball, receive closer to the centre and then continue forward. He could also move beyond the defensive line after combining with Olmo.

His 42nd-minute goal illustrated the value of this relationship. Olmo supplied the final pass, and Raphinha finished the attack from a central position.

The sequence was consistent with the broader pattern: Raphinha was not simply receiving wide and crossing or carrying the ball from the flank. He was participating in central combinations and then attacking the box.

### Opponent Response

Racing had to account for a Barcelona attacker appearing in central areas without losing the width around the attack.

That made the defensive reference points less stable.

If a defender followed Raphinha inside, space could open elsewhere. If the defensive line maintained its shape, Raphinha could continue operating between or around the central defenders.

The article does not establish that every movement produced the same defensive response, but the repeated central positioning clearly changed the problem Racing had to solve.

### Consequence

Barcelona could create attacks from more than one reference point.

Raphinha’s central movement gave Olmo a nearby attacking partner while the wide players continued to stretch the defensive structure.

That combination helped Barcelona sustain pressure around the final third and penalty area.

# TACTICAL EVIDENCE

## 1. Raphinha’s first goal came from central attacking involvement

Raphinha converted Barcelona’s opening penalty after being fouled.

The sequence placed him in a central attacking context rather than requiring him to create the action from the left wing.

That was significant because his role throughout the match increasingly resembled that of a mobile central attacker.

## 2. The open-play goal showed the Olmo–Raphinha connection

Raphinha’s second goal came after Dani Olmo played him into the attack.

The relationship between the two players was important.

Olmo could operate as the connector while Raphinha moved beyond him and attacked the space closer to goal.

This gave Barcelona a direct midfield-to-attack connection without requiring Raphinha to remain wide.

## 3. Raphinha assisted Yamal’s chance

In the second half, Raphinha supplied the pass for Lamine Yamal’s shot that struck the post.

The sequence again showed Raphinha influencing the attack from a position closer to the centre.

His role was therefore not limited to finishing attacks. He also helped create them for players arriving from other attacking positions.

## 4. Raphinha’s movement supported multiple attacking routes

Raphinha’s central role allowed Barcelona to combine through the middle while still using wide players.

That is the key distinction.

The tactical value was not simply moving a winger into the centre. It was moving him centrally **without removing width from the rest of the attack**.

# IMPORTANT ADJUSTMENTS

## Barcelona changed the function without abandoning the structure

Barcelona did not need to completely redesign the 4-3-3 to make Raphinha more central.

Instead, the positional relationships within the structure changed.

Raphinha moved inside.

Olmo connected play.

Yamal and Adeyemi could maintain wider attacking positions.

The result was a flexible attacking structure in which the nominal positions did not always describe the actual locations of the players during attacks.

Barcelona also made personnel changes as the match progressed. The goalkeeper changed at half-time, midfield changes followed later, and Raphinha was replaced by Gabriel Jesus in the second half.

Those substitutions changed the personnel available to Barcelona, but the main tactical feature of the first part of the match remained Raphinha’s central movement.

# KEY PLAYER: RAPHINHA

Raphinha was the clearest example of Barcelona’s positional flexibility.

His hat-trick gave him the most visible contribution, but the tactical value of his performance extended beyond finishing.

He acted as a mobile central reference point, connected with Olmo, attacked the penalty area and contributed to Yamal’s attacking opportunity.

The performance therefore illustrates a broader coaching principle:

**A player’s starting position does not necessarily define his attacking function.**

A winger can become a central attacker without the team formally changing its base formation, provided the surrounding players can maintain the width and connections required by the structure.

# MATCH MOMENT

The clearest pattern was the repeated appearance of Raphinha in central attacking areas.

Rather than treating the left-wing position as a fixed location, Barcelona used it as a starting reference.

Raphinha could move inside.

Olmo could connect.

That combination made Barcelona’s attack less dependent on a single fixed forward position.

# CONCLUSION

Raphinha’s performance against Racing Santander was a useful example of the difference between formation and function.

Barcelona remained in a 4-3-3, but Raphinha did not behave like a conventional left winger for the entire match.

His movement into central areas gave Barcelona another link between midfield and attack and another player capable of arriving in the penalty area.

The important tactical mechanism was therefore the relationship between movement and structure.

Raphinha could move centrally because Barcelona still had players capable of occupying the wider spaces. Olmo could connect play around him, while Yamal and Adeyemi could maintain the width that prevented the attack from becoming too narrow.

The result was an attacking structure in which the starting formation provided the framework, but player movement determined how that framework functioned.

# TGO ANALYST VIEW

Raphinha’s central role was a key feature of Barcelona’s attacking performance against Racing Santander.

The match demonstrated that a 4-3-3 does not require its nominal wingers to remain permanently wide. Raphinha’s movement created additional central connections while Barcelona retained width through the surrounding players.

The broader tactical lesson is straightforward:

**Formation describes the starting structure; movement determines how that structure actually attacks.**
