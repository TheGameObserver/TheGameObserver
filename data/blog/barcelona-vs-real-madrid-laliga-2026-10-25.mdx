Barcelona had periods of attacking pressure, but Real Madrid remained dangerous whenever the opportunity to move forward appeared. Madrid's 2–1 victory at Camp Nou highlighted the risk of committing players forward without sufficient protection behind the ball.

## Madrid's threat beyond possession

Madrid did not need to dominate the ball to influence the match. Their defensive organisation gave them a platform to remain compact, while their forward players looked for opportunities to attack the spaces Barcelona left during possession.

Kylian Mbappé opened the scoring in the 41st minute. Barcelona responded through Raphinha in the 61st minute, bringing the match back into balance.

## The decisive transition

The winning goal arrived in the 87th minute. Jude Bellingham combined with Arda Güler, who connected with Eduardo Camavinga. Camavinga then created the opportunity for Mbappé to score his second goal.

The sequence showed the value of quick, coordinated forward movement. Madrid moved from midfield progression to a decisive final action, punishing Barcelona at a moment when the home side were still searching for a winner.

## Key Tactical Findings

- Madrid remained dangerous without needing sustained possession.
- Barcelona's attacking commitment created transition risks.
- Bellingham, Güler and Camavinga connected the decisive move.
- Mbappé's two goals settled the Clásico.

## TGO Analyst View

The match underlined an important distinction: possession can help a team control territory, but it does not automatically protect the spaces behind its attack. Madrid's ability to move forward quickly and connect their midfield with the final action proved decisive.

## Conclusion

Madrid won by making their attacking moments count. Barcelona had periods of pressure and restored parity, but Madrid retained a threat in transition and used one decisive sequence to secure the result.
