## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Real Madrid defeated Elche 3–2 at the Martínez Valero after establishing a 2–0 half-time lead, surrendering that advantage in the second half and recovering it through Carlos Espí in stoppage time. The most significant feature of the match was therefore not the late winner, but the contrast between Madrid's first-half control and their second-half loss of control.

Elche began with an aggressive approach and looked to defend through pressure and possession. Madrid repeatedly found ways beyond that pressure, using their technical quality, width and forward-running threats to create dangerous situations. Yan Diomande's right-sided involvement was particularly important in the second goal, when his cross found an unmarked Mbappé.

After half-time, however, Elche became increasingly capable of sustaining possession and advancing attacks. Their substitutions added fresh attacking connections, while Madrid's intensity and territorial control declined. The second half therefore developed under very different conditions from the first.

## MATCH CONTEXT

The match was Matchday 6 of the 2026/27 LaLiga season and was played at the Martínez Valero on 15 September 2026.

Real Madrid arrived having won four of their opening five league matches. Elche had yet to win, but had drawn with Athletic Club in their previous fixture. Before the match, Elche manager Martín Anselmi described his preferred approach as defending through possession while maintaining defensive intensity.

Real Madrid started in a 4-2-3-1, with Thibaut Courtois behind Trent Alexander-Arnold, Ibrahima Konaté, Dean Huijsen and Marc Cucurella. Aurélien Tchouaméni and Federico Valverde formed the midfield base, with Arda Güler, Yan Diomande and Vinícius Júnior behind Kylian Mbappé.

Elche used a 4-5-1, with Abiel Osorio as the central forward and a midfield five behind him.

## TACTICAL SETUP

Elche's starting structure was designed to combine pressure without completely abandoning central midfield coverage.

Osorio provided the highest reference point, while the midfield five could compress the central areas and support the first line of pressure. Their intention was consistent with Anselmi's pre-match description: possession was not simply an attacking tool, but part of their defensive method.

Madrid's 4-2-3-1 gave them several mechanisms for escaping that pressure.

Tchouaméni and Valverde could provide the first connection from the defensive line, while Güler operated between the lines. Diomande and Vinícius offered immediate width and depth, with Mbappé providing the most advanced reference.

This gave Madrid multiple ways to attack Elche's pressure: they could circulate through the double pivot, find Güler between lines, release the wide attackers, or play forward toward Mbappé.

The central tactical question was therefore straightforward:

**Could Elche's pressure force Madrid into predictable or rushed possession before Madrid could exploit the spaces created by that pressure?**

For much of the first half, Madrid won that battle.

## KEY TACTICAL BATTLE

### Problem: Elche wanted to press, but pressing Madrid created space behind the pressure

Elche started aggressively. Real Madrid's official report noted that the hosts pressed high, but could not prevent Madrid from finding the attacking areas they wanted.

This is the fundamental tension of a high press against a team with Madrid's attacking profiles.

When Elche pushed forward, the distance between their defensive line and the first pressing line became important. Madrid had players capable of attacking those spaces immediately.

Vinícius could threaten from the left, Diomande could attack the opposite channel, and Mbappé could run beyond the defensive line.

The objective was not simply to "beat the press." It was to make Elche pay for the space their press created.

### Structure: Madrid used width to stretch Elche's defensive line

Madrid's attacking structure gave particular importance to the wide channels.

Diomande's involvement in the second goal is the clearest example. He progressed down the right and delivered the cross from which Mbappé finished to make it 2–0.

The sequence matters because Elche's pressure had not simply disappeared. Madrid had created an attacking situation in which their wide player could advance beyond the initial pressure and deliver into the penalty area.

Mbappé's movement then became decisive.

The striker was able to attack the central finishing zone while Diomande provided the final delivery from outside.

The mechanism was therefore:

**Elche press → Madrid escape → wide progression → penalty-area occupation → final action.**

### Movement: Güler and the front three gave Madrid multiple progression routes

Madrid's attacking four were not restricted to one fixed lane.

Güler could receive between the lines, Vinícius could attack from the left, Diomande could provide width on the right and Mbappé could move across the last line.

That made it difficult for Elche to maintain a single defensive reference.

The first goal also came from a different route. Güler's direct free-kick struck the post before rebounding into the goal off goalkeeper Matías Dituro.

Madrid subsequently created further opportunities from open play. Vinícius forced a save after receiving from Mbappé, while Güler went close after a fast break.

The 2–0 score therefore reflected more than the two goals themselves: Madrid were repeatedly reaching dangerous areas.

### Opponent response: Elche changed the conditions after half-time

The crucial change came after the interval.

Elche did not need to abandon their possession-based approach. Instead, they began to sustain possession for longer periods and move Madrid's defensive block around the pitch. Elche were increasingly able to control the ball and territorial rhythm after the break.

### Consequence: Madrid's attacking advantage remained, but their control disappeared

This distinction explains the final score better than simply describing Elche's comeback as a "fightback."

Madrid continued to create chances. Vinícius missed narrowly, Mbappé had an effort blocked, Diomande had another attempt blocked and Madrid eventually produced the winning attack.

But their ability to control the match without the ball deteriorated.

Madrid's second-half problem was that the transition between their attacking pressure and their defensive organisation became increasingly uncomfortable. As Elche sustained longer attacking phases, Madrid found it harder to dictate where the game was being played.

## TACTICAL EVIDENCE

### 1. Madrid's first-half superiority came from attacking the spaces behind Elche's pressure

Real Madrid's official match report noted that Elche pressed high but were unable to prevent Madrid from finding the attacking areas they wanted.

The match events support that assessment.

Madrid opened the scoring through Güler's free-kick, which struck the post before rebounding into the goal off Matías Dituro. Mbappé then doubled the lead after Diomande progressed on the right and supplied the cross.

Madrid continued to threaten before the interval, with Vinícius forcing a save, Güler going close after a fast break and Vinícius narrowly missing another opportunity.

The important point was therefore not simply that Madrid scored twice. They repeatedly converted their ability to escape Elche's pressure into forward attacks.

### 2. Diomande gave Madrid a direct route beyond the press

Diomande's role was particularly valuable because he gave Madrid an immediate vertical outlet on the right.

His assist for Mbappé came after he advanced into a dangerous position and delivered across the penalty area. Once the first line of pressure had been bypassed, Elche's defensive line was forced to turn and defend its own goal.

Diomande therefore gave Madrid more than width. He provided a direct route from the first phase of possession into the final third.

### 3. Anselmi's substitutions increased Elche's attacking options

Elche introduced Thomas Lemar and Lucas Cepeda at 57 minutes, followed by Fer Niño and Rubén Sánchez at 68, and Josan at 80.

The changes did not automatically cause the comeback, but they gave Elche additional attacking profiles around the attack.

Cepeda directly assisted Osorio.

Josan directly assisted Fer Niño.

The connections between the substitutions and the goals are clear, while the broader improvement remained collective rather than attributable to one substitution alone.

### 4. Madrid's response increased attacking threat but did not immediately restore control

Mourinho introduced Jude Bellingham and Brahim Díaz for Güler and Vinícius at 68 minutes, then Endrick for Tchouaméni and Carlos Espí for Trent Alexander-Arnold later in the match.

The changes increased Madrid's attacking options.

But they also altered the balance of the team.

Tchouaméni was replaced after Elche had already equalised, so his removal cannot be presented as the cause of the 2–2 goal. More broadly, the available match evidence does not establish that any individual substitution directly caused the equaliser.

What can be established is that Madrid were already struggling to control the game when the later changes were made, and Elche had already turned the match into a more sustained attacking contest.

### 5. The final winner came when Madrid attacked the space behind Elche again

After Elche had levelled, Madrid recovered some of the directness that had characterised the first half.

Mbappé raced in behind the Elche defence and squared for Espí, who finished from close range in stoppage time.

The winner was tactically significant because it returned Madrid to the mechanism that had worked earlier:

**vertical running + space behind the defensive line + Mbappé's ability to exploit it.**

The final attack therefore restored the lead through a familiar Madrid strength, even though it did not erase the second-half control problem.

## IMPORTANT ADJUSTMENTS

### Initial situation

Elche began with a high press and a 4-5-1 structure designed to compress central spaces and make Madrid's build-up uncomfortable.

Madrid's technical quality and attacking depth repeatedly allowed them to escape that pressure.

### Adjustment

After half-time, Elche increased their ability to sustain possession and introduced fresh attacking profiles.

Thomas Lemar and Lucas Cepeda entered at 57 minutes, followed by Fer Niño and Rubén Sánchez at 68, and Josan at 80.

### Opponent response

Madrid introduced Bellingham and Brahim Díaz at 68 minutes, then Endrick and Espí later.

These changes increased attacking threat, but the team did not immediately regain the territorial control it had enjoyed before half-time.

### Effect

Elche scored twice to turn 0–2 into 2–2.

Madrid then recovered its vertical threat late in the game, with Mbappé assisting Espí for the winner.

The match therefore contained two distinct tactical adjustments: Elche improved their attacking continuity, while Madrid attempted to restore threat through personnel and greater attacking presence.

## KEY PLAYER: YAN DIOMANDE

Diomande's importance was not limited to recording an assist.

His starting position on the right gave Madrid a direct route into the space created when Elche pushed forward.

His cross for Mbappé's goal demonstrated the value of that outlet. Later, he remained involved in Madrid's attacking threat, providing the assist for a blocked Mbappé effort in the closing stages.

The wider tactical value was his ability to make Elche's defensive line defend both the ball and the space behind it.

That dual threat helped Madrid punish the initial high press.

## MATCH MOMENT: ELCHE'S EQUALISER

The 83rd-minute equaliser was the clearest indication that the match had changed.

Josan, introduced shortly beforehand, supplied Fer Niño, who finished from the right side of the penalty area to make it 2–2.

The significance of the goal was not simply the finish itself. It came during a period in which Elche had increasingly established possession and attacking territory.

Madrid had spent the first half forcing Elche to defend.

Now Elche were forcing Madrid to defend.

## WHAT COULD HAVE CHANGED?

The clearest alternative for Madrid was not necessarily another attacking substitution.

It was greater control of the game after taking the 2–0 lead.

Mourinho's post-match assessment also centred on Madrid's loss of control rather than simply the goals they conceded. That distinction is important.

Madrid did not need to prevent Elche from producing every attacking moment. They needed to maintain enough territorial and positional control to prevent Elche from repeatedly establishing attacks.

Exactly how that should have been achieved — through a deeper block, more controlled possession, different pressing triggers or different midfield profiles — cannot be established conclusively from the match evidence alone.

## TOURNAMENT / WIDER IMPLICATIONS

The result continued Madrid's unbeaten league start, while Elche remained without a league victory after six matches.

More relevant tactically, the match provides an early illustration of the demands of Madrid's developing defensive model.

The first half showed that Madrid could use aggressive pressure, technical security and attacking depth to make Elche's high press difficult to sustain. The second half exposed the difficulty of maintaining that control once the opponent established longer periods of possession and territorial pressure.

That is an important distinction for evaluating the developing team: the question is not simply whether Madrid can press, but whether they can move between high pressure, mid-block defending and controlled possession without surrendering territorial authority.

## CONCLUSION

Real Madrid's 3–2 victory at Elche was decided by a late attacking action, but the match itself was defined by a much broader tactical contrast.

In the first half, Madrid repeatedly exploited Elche's high press. Their 4-2-3-1 gave them multiple routes beyond the first defensive line, while Diomande's width, Mbappé's depth and Güler's positioning helped turn pressure into attacking opportunities.

After half-time, that mechanism became less consistent.

Elche sustained possession, introduced fresh attacking players and increasingly moved Madrid away from the territorial control they had established. Two goals turned a 2–0 deficit into 2–2.

Madrid ultimately won because they rediscovered one of their most dangerous mechanisms: attacking space behind the defensive line. Mbappé's run and assist allowed Espí to restore the lead in stoppage time.

But the central lesson is not that Madrid found a dramatic way to win.

It is that **their first-half control was much more convincing than their second-half management of the game**.

The scoreline says Real Madrid won despite losing control of the match.

The tactical story says they still have to learn how to keep control once the opponent changes the conditions.

## TGO ANALYST VIEW

**The match may reveal a distinction between territorial control, chance-generation control and game-state control. Madrid appeared to control the first half, lost part of that control in the second, yet retained enough attacking threat to win late.**
