## Executive Summary

France’s victory over Belgium was a reminder that controlling a match and unlocking it are two different challenges.

Belgium defended effectively and took the lead through Dodi Lukébakio. France struggled to turn its attacking pressure into goals until Désiré Doué, Rayan Cherki and Michael Olise helped transform the final stages.

Doué equalised, Cherki put France ahead, and Olise finished the comeback with two goals.

The decisive story was not possession alone. It was France finding the individual quality and attacking variety that had been missing earlier.

## Match Context

France defeated Belgium 4–1 at the Stade de France on 5 October 2026 in the UEFA Nations League.

Belgium led at half-time after Lukébakio exploited space behind France’s defence. For much of the match, the visitors’ compact organisation made it difficult for France to create a breakthrough.

Then the game changed dramatically. France scored four times in the closing stages, turning a frustrating contest into a convincing win.

## The Tactical Problem: Possession Without Penetration

France had the ball, but Belgium made the spaces that mattered difficult to access.

A compact defensive block can allow an opponent to circulate possession while protecting the central areas around the penalty box. The attacking team may appear in control without consistently creating dangerous opportunities.

That was France’s challenge. More possession was not the answer by itself; France needed to create different problems for Belgium’s defenders.

Belgium also demonstrated the risk of France pushing forward. Lukébakio’s opening goal came when Roméo Lavia sent a long pass behind the French defensive line, allowing the forward to run through and finish.

The sequence exposed the trade-off: France needed to commit players forward to break down Belgium, but doing so could leave space for the visitors to attack.

## The Turning Point: Different Attacking Options

France’s response came partly through its bench.

The introductions of Olise and Cherki, followed by Doué, gave France additional ways to threaten in the final third. Their importance was not simply fresh legs; each could contribute through individual creation, combination play or decisive finishing.

Doué broke the deadlock in the closing stages with a shot from outside the box.

Cherki then put France ahead after an Olise cross. Olise followed with two goals of his own to complete the turnaround.

The finishes came through different actions rather than one repeated pattern. That variety made France harder to contain and helped turn sustained pressure into an outcome.

The timing strongly supports the importance of the substitutes, although it does not prove that the changes alone caused every goal. Belgium’s defensive response, the changing scoreline and the quality of France’s execution also mattered.

## Key Player: Michael Olise

Olise became the central figure of France’s comeback.

He created Cherki’s goal before scoring twice himself. His contribution demonstrated the value of having a player who can both supply a teammate and finish an attack.

Rather than claiming that Olise single-handedly changed France’s entire structure, the clearest conclusion is more specific: **he directly influenced the decisive phase of the match through creation and finishing.**

## The Wider Lesson

Belgium’s lead showed that a well-organised defence can frustrate a team even when it spends long periods under pressure.

France’s comeback showed the other side of that problem. Attacking depth matters when the initial approach is not producing enough penetration.

The lesson is not that substitutions guarantee a turnaround. It is that different attacking profiles can give a team new solutions when its existing approach becomes predictable or ineffective.

## Conclusion

France did not win simply because it had controlled the ball. It won because, late in the match, it began producing the decisive actions that had been missing.

Belgium’s organisation held for much of the contest, and its direct attack created the opening goal. But France’s attacking changes brought a different level of threat, with Doué, Cherki and Olise turning the game around.

**Possession created the platform. Attacking variety and execution decided the match.**

## Key Takeaways

- Belgium’s compact defence made France’s possession difficult to convert into goals.
- Lukébakio’s goal showed the danger of leaving space behind an advancing defence.
- France’s substitutes added decisive attacking quality in the closing stages.
- Olise influenced the comeback through both creation and finishing.
- The match illustrates the value of attacking depth without proving that substitutions alone determine results.
