## Executive Summary

Inter recovered from an early 2–0 deficit to defeat Udinese 5–3 at San Siro, but the decisive tactical shift was visible before half-time. Cristian Chivu kept the 3-5-2, yet Inter increased the speed of their circulation, raised the intensity of their pressure and varied the heights of their attacking players.

That altered the relationships inside the structure. Carlos Augusto operated high and wide on the left, Andy Diouf provided more aggressive depth on the opposite side, Nicolò Barella became increasingly involved beyond the first midfield line, and Marcus Thuram varied his positioning rather than remaining fixed alongside Pio Esposito.

Inter finished with 66% possession, 27 shots, 12 on target and 10 corners, while Udinese had 34% possession, six shots, five on target and one corner. The numbers reflect the territorial shift, but the more important change was how Inter used their existing structure.

## Match Context

Inter entered Matchday 4 with three Serie A victories from three matches. The match also followed Inter's Champions League defeat to Real Madrid, and Chivu rotated his team, with John Stones making his first start for the club.

Udinese immediately disrupted Inter's rhythm. James Abankwah headed the visitors ahead in the ninth minute from an Unai Gómez cross, while Jurgen Ekkelenkamp doubled the advantage in the 16th minute after Keinan Davis created the opening.

Inter responded through Carlos Augusto in the 26th minute and Barella in the 35th, restoring parity before half-time. Thuram put Inter ahead in the 59th minute, Pio Esposito made it 4–2 in the 69th, and Ange-Yoan Bonny added a fifth in the 81st. Idrissa Gueye scored Udinese's late consolation in the 93rd.

The scoreline therefore contained two distinct phases: an opening period in which Udinese repeatedly found routes forward, followed by a long period in which Inter controlled territory and attacking volume.

## Tactical Setup

Both teams began with back-three structures.

Inter used a 3-5-2 with Josep Martínez behind Manuel Akanji, John Stones and Alessandro Bastoni; Andy Diouf, Barella, Piotr Zielinski, Henrikh Mkhitaryan and Carlos Augusto formed the midfield line, with Thuram and Esposito up front.

Udinese used a 3-4-2-1: Maduka Okoye behind Abankwah, Christian Kabasele and Enzo Ebosse; Mërgim Vojvoda and Hassane Kamara supplied width, Jesper Karlström and Lennon Miller formed the central midfield, and Ekkelenkamp and Unai Gómez operated behind Davis.

The structural matchup was relatively symmetrical at the back but different in the next line. Udinese's two attacking midfielders could look for spaces between Inter's midfield and defensive lines, while Davis provided a reference point for forward play.

Inter's numerical presence in midfield became more useful as their players began operating at different heights.

## Key Tactical Battle

### Problem: Inter's structure was present, but its initial tempo was too slow

Inter did not lack a tactical structure. The early problem was how that structure functioned.

Udinese found routes through wide areas and between Inter's midfield and defensive lines. Their first goal came from Unai Gómez's delivery toward Abankwah, while the second developed from a quick combination involving Davis and Ekkelenkamp.

The issue was therefore not simply the 3-5-2. Inter were initially circulating without enough speed or pressure around the ball to prevent Udinese from progressing.

### Structure: Inter retained the 3-5-2 but created more varied heights

Inter's response did not require a nominal formation change. The important change was in the relationships inside it.

Carlos Augusto increasingly operated high and wide on the left, while Diouf provided a more direct depth threat on the right. Barella became more active beyond the first midfield line, while Thuram varied his positioning and could drop toward the ball.

The result was a less predictable attacking structure: some players supported circulation, others stretched the pitch, and others attacked the spaces created between them.

### Movement: Thuram changed the reference points

Thuram did not remain permanently alongside Esposito. His movement toward the ball created moments in which Udinese's defenders had to decide whether to follow him or protect the space behind.

That movement was important in the 2–2 sequence, when Thuram's involvement helped create the conditions for Barella's finish.

The tactical value was not that Thuram always dropped, but that his changing depth prevented Udinese's back line from defending against two fixed forward references.

### Opponent response: Udinese's midfield faced more difficult decisions

As Inter increased the speed of circulation and varied player heights, Udinese's midfielders had to manage more simultaneous threats.

Carlos Augusto's high position stretched the defensive structure horizontally, while Diouf's forward movement supplied depth. Barella could then arrive in more advanced areas without needing to remain on the same line as the other midfielders.

Udinese increasingly had to choose between protecting the centre and conceding space outside, or protecting the width and opening central access.

### Consequence: possession became territorial pressure

Inter's possession became more threatening because it was accompanied by movement, width and greater pressure after turnovers.

By the second half, Udinese spent longer defending closer to their own penalty area. The key was not simply that Inter had more of the ball, but that the ball was circulating while Inter occupied more useful positions.

## Tactical Evidence

### The turnaround began before the half-time break

Inter were already back at 2–2 before half-time. Carlos Augusto reduced the deficit in the 26th minute and Barella equalised in the 35th.

That is important evidence for the article's thesis: Inter had altered the game without needing a major formation change or waiting for a wholesale second-half restructuring.

### Carlos Augusto became a width-and-height reference

Carlos Augusto's positioning gave Inter a consistently high and wide option on the left. That increased the horizontal distance Udinese had to defend and created room for central players to move.

His goal also demonstrated the attacking value of that position, but his larger contribution was the space his positioning created for the rest of the structure.

### Diouf supplied the opposite movement

On the right, Diouf provided a more aggressive depth threat. This gave Inter complementary attacking behaviours rather than identical wing-back positioning.

For the 3–2, Inter progressed through Diouf on the right before he supplied Thuram for the finish in the 59th minute. The sequence illustrated how width and depth could combine without Inter abandoning their 3-5-2.

### Barella connected midfield to attack

Barella's influence extended beyond his goal. Inter's official post-match analysis highlighted his involvement in recovering the ball for the 2–2 sequence and launching Diouf in the build-up to the 3–2.

His movement beyond the first midfield line also became more effective as Thuram varied his depth.

### Post-loss pressure increased the cost of Udinese's recoveries

Inter's pressure after losing possession became more aggressive as the match progressed. This reduced Udinese's ability to turn recoveries into controlled forward attacks.

It did not eliminate the threat: Gueye's 93rd-minute goal showed that Inter's defensive transition control remained imperfect. But the balance of the match had already shifted substantially toward Inter.

## Important Adjustments

### Initial Situation

Udinese's 3-4-2-1 initially found routes through Davis, Ekkelenkamp and Gómez, while Inter's circulation was comparatively measured.

### Adjustment

Inter increased the speed of circulation and the intensity of their reaction after losing possession. Within the existing 3-5-2, Carlos Augusto operated high, Diouf attacked depth, Barella moved into more advanced areas and Thuram varied his positioning.

### Opponent Response

Udinese's midfielders and defenders increasingly had to defend different heights and widths simultaneously. Their early routes forward became less frequent as Inter pushed them deeper.

### Effect

Inter moved from being vulnerable to Udinese's transitions to controlling the territory in which the match was played. Later substitutions reinforced that control without requiring a fundamental change to the nominal formation.

## Key Player: Carlos Augusto

Carlos Augusto's importance extended beyond his goal. His high positioning changed the geometry of Inter's attack.

By remaining high and wide, he forced Udinese to account for a player closer to the final third while creating additional room for Inter's central players.

The key tactical point is that Carlos Augusto functioned as both a wing-back and a positional reference. His location influenced where Udinese could safely defend.

## Match Moment: Inter's 3–2

The third goal captured the tactical change clearly.

In the 59th minute, Inter progressed through Diouf on the right, with the wing-back providing the final ball for Thuram to finish.

The sequence reflected several elements that had become more pronounced since the opening phase: faster progression, a wing-back attacking depth, width creating access to the final third and Thuram arriving in a dangerous finishing position.

## What Could Have Changed?

Udinese's strongest period came when they could progress quickly through Davis and find Ekkelenkamp or Gómez in useful positions between Inter's midfield and defensive lines.

A credible alternative would therefore have required Udinese to preserve those connections for longer once Inter increased their pressure.

However, the available evidence does not establish a single tactical adjustment that would certainly have changed the result. Inter's increased territorial control simply reduced the frequency with which Udinese could reproduce the situations that had produced their first two goals.

Runjaić's later substitutions introduced fresh personnel, but by then Inter had established a substantial territorial advantage.

## The Larger Tactical Lesson

The match demonstrates why formation labels alone are insufficient to explain tactical change.

Inter played 3-5-2, yet the same nominal formation produced two different performances. Early on, the structure was comparatively slow and vulnerable when Udinese progressed quickly. Later, the structure became more fluid: one wing-back stayed high, the opposite side supplied depth, midfielders moved through different vertical zones, Thuram varied his depth, and the team reacted more aggressively after losing possession.

The formation did not change. The relationships inside it did.

## Conclusion

Inter's 5–3 victory was not simply a story of recovering from a two-goal deficit. The more significant tactical development was the transformation of the team's existing 3-5-2.

Udinese initially exploited a slower Inter by finding wide areas and useful positions between the lines. Inter responded without abandoning their basic structure. Instead, they increased the speed of possession, improved their occupation of the width, varied the heights of their midfielders and forwards, and became more aggressive immediately after losing the ball.

That combination gradually removed the conditions that had made Udinese dangerous.

By the second half, Inter were no longer simply trying to recover the score. They were controlling where the match was being played.

The three goals conceded still expose a defensive issue, particularly in moments of transition, but the comeback showed how a team can alter the rhythm and positional relationships of a game without changing its nominal formation.

**Inter did not solve the match by changing the shape. They solved it by making the shape move faster.**

## Key Takeaways

1. **Inter's comeback began through a change in tempo and relationships, not a nominal formation change.**
2. **Carlos Augusto's high positioning stretched Udinese horizontally and created additional space for central players.**
3. **Thuram's changing depth helped create different reference points for Udinese's back three.**
4. **Diouf's forward movement gave Inter a complementary depth threat on the opposite side.**
5. **Barella connected midfield and attack while also contributing to Inter's pressure and progression.**
6. **More aggressive post-loss pressure reduced Udinese's ability to reproduce the transition situations that had hurt Inter early.**
7. **The 5–3 also showed that Inter's defensive transition management remained less stable than their attacking structure.**

## TGO Analyst View

* Both teams used three-centre-back structures, creating a tactical contest around width, midfield occupation and the spaces around the opposing midfield.
* Udinese's 3-4-2-1 used Ekkelenkamp and Gómez behind Davis, while Inter's 3-5-2 supplied an additional central midfielder and two forwards.
* Inter's improvement was associated with faster circulation, more aggressive post-loss pressure, higher positioning from Carlos Augusto and increased forward movement from Diouf.
* Barella and Thuram developed an important positional relationship, with Thuram varying his depth and Barella making more advanced movements.
* Inter's attacking structure became more fluid without requiring a nominal formation change.
* Udinese's early attacking success came from direct progression, Davis' involvement and access to areas between Inter's lines.

The useful analytical question is therefore not simply why Inter came back from 2–0 down, but **what changed inside the same nominal structure that allowed them to take control of the match.**
