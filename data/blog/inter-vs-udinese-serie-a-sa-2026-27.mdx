## Executive Summary

Inter recovered from an early 2–0 deficit to defeat Udinese 5–3 at San Siro, but the decisive tactical shift was already visible before half-time. Cristian Chivu kept his 3-5-2, yet Inter became faster in possession, more aggressive immediately after losing the ball and more expansive through Carlos Augusto and Andy Diouf.

That change altered the relationships inside the structure. Barella moved into more advanced areas, Marcus Thuram repeatedly dropped away from the defensive line, and the wing-backs increasingly stretched Udinese's back three. Inter went from a relatively predictable circulation pattern to a fluid attacking structure that repeatedly created central and wide access.

Inter finished with 66% possession, 27 shots, 12 on target and 10 corners, while Udinese had 34% possession, six shots and one corner. The comeback therefore reflected more than individual finishing; Inter progressively changed the conditions under which the game was being played.

## Match Context

Inter entered Matchday 4 with three Serie A victories from three matches, while Udinese had also made a positive opening to the campaign. The match followed Inter's Champions League defeat to Real Madrid, and Chivu rotated his team, including John Stones for his first start and Pio Esposito alongside Marcus Thuram.

At San Siro, Udinese immediately disrupted Inter's rhythm. James Abankwah headed the visitors ahead in the ninth minute from an Unai Gómez delivery, while Jurgen Ekkelenkamp doubled the advantage seven minutes later after Keinan Davis helped create the opening.

Inter responded through Carlos Augusto in the 26th minute and Barella in the 35th, restoring parity before half-time. Thuram then completed the turnaround in the 57th minute, Pio Esposito made it 4–2 in the 67th and Ange-Yoan Bonny added a fifth in the 79th. Idrissa Gueye scored a late consolation.

The scoreline therefore concealed two substantially different phases: an opening period in which Udinese repeatedly found ways through Inter's defensive structure, followed by a long period in which Inter controlled territory and attacking volume.

## Tactical Setup

Both teams began with a back-three structure.

Inter used a 3-5-2 with Manuel Akanji, John Stones and Alessandro Bastoni behind Andy Diouf, Nicolò Barella, Piotr Zielinski, Henrikh Mkhitaryan and Carlos Augusto. Thuram and Esposito formed the front two.

Udinese used a 3-4-2-1. Abankwah, Christian Kabasele and Enzo Ebosse formed the back three, with Mërgim Vojvoda and Hassane Kamara providing width. Jesper Karlström and Lennon Miller occupied central midfield, while Ekkelenkamp and Gómez operated behind Davis.

The structural matchup was relatively symmetrical at the back but different in the next line. Udinese's two attacking midfielders had a clear role between Inter's midfield and defensive lines, while Davis provided a reference point capable of receiving forward passes and attacking depth.

Inter had an extra central midfielder and two forwards, but that numerical presence only became meaningful when the midfielders and forwards moved at different heights.

## Key Tactical Battle

### Problem: Inter's structure was present, but its initial tempo was too slow

Inter did not begin without a tactical structure. The problem was how that structure was functioning.

Udinese attacked the spaces around Inter's wing-back positions and between the midfield and defensive lines. The opening goal came from a wide situation involving Gómez and Abankwah, while the second developed from a quick combination that allowed Ekkelenkamp to receive dangerously.

Inter's three centre-backs were therefore asked to defend forward-running threats while the wing-backs managed Udinese's width.

The issue was not simply the 3-5-2. The structure was initially operating with insufficient speed and pressure around the ball.

### Structure: Inter retained the 3-5-2 but created more varied heights

The response did not require Chivu to abandon the system. Inter altered the relationships inside it.

Bastoni became increasingly involved in construction, while Barella and Mkhitaryan could drop into supporting positions. Carlos Augusto stayed higher and wider on the left, while Diouf increasingly attacked depth on the opposite side.

Inter's players were no longer occupying the same horizontal line. Some supported circulation, others stretched the pitch, while Barella and Thuram attacked the spaces created between those movements.

### Movement: Thuram's dropping movement changed the reference points

Thuram did not remain permanently alongside Esposito. At different moments he dropped toward the ball, creating uncertainty for Udinese's centre-backs.

That movement could pull a defender away from the defensive line and open a lane for Barella's forward movement. The equaliser illustrated the connection: Thuram controlled the ball inside the area before setting up Barella for the finish.

Rather than treating the forwards as two fixed targets, Inter increasingly used one forward to manipulate the defensive line while midfielders attacked the spaces around him.

### Opponent response: Udinese's midfield began to lose control

As Inter increased the speed of circulation and movement, Udinese's central midfielders faced more difficult decisions.

They had to respond to Inter's midfielders arriving at different heights while their back three also accounted for Thuram and Esposito. Carlos Augusto's high position and Diouf's forward movement created width and depth simultaneously.

Udinese therefore faced a choice between protecting the centre and conceding space outside, or protecting the width and opening central lanes.

### Consequence: Inter turned possession into territorial pressure

Inter's possession became more threatening because it was accompanied by movement, width and immediate pressure after turnovers.

By the second half, Udinese were spending longer periods defending their own penalty-area region. The key was not simply that Inter had more of the ball, but that the ball was being circulated under increasingly favourable positional conditions.

## Tactical Evidence

### The first-half turnaround came before any major structural substitution

Inter were already back at 2–2 before half-time. Carlos Augusto reduced the deficit in the 26th minute, while Barella equalised in the 35th after Thuram's involvement.

The tactical shift therefore began while the original XI and 3-5-2 were still on the pitch. The evidence points toward a change in how Inter used the existing structure rather than an immediate formation change.

### Carlos Augusto became a width-and-height problem

Carlos Augusto increasingly operated high on the left rather than constantly dropping alongside the midfield. That forced Udinese's defensive structure to account for a player closer to the final third.

His positioning increased the horizontal distance Udinese had to cover and created room for Inter's central players to operate.

### Diouf provided the opposite movement

On the right, Diouf increasingly attacked the depth. This gave Inter a complementary movement pattern: Carlos Augusto maintained width and height on one side, while Diouf attacked space more aggressively on the other.

In the 3–2 goal, Diouf advanced into the right channel and reached the byline before cutting the ball back for Thuram.

### Barella connected midfield to attack

Barella became increasingly important as Inter's midfield moved higher. His goal was one contribution, but his larger tactical value was his ability to arrive beyond the first midfield line.

With Thuram capable of dropping toward the ball, Barella could attack the space that movement created.

### Inter's post-loss pressure increased the cost of Udinese's recoveries

The change in tempo was also visible immediately after turnovers. Increased aggression after losing possession reduced Udinese's opportunity to convert recoveries into controlled transitions.

This did not eliminate Udinese's threat — the late Gueye goal demonstrated that Inter's defensive control remained imperfect — but it substantially changed the balance of the match.

## Important Adjustments

### Initial situation

Udinese's 3-4-2-1 initially accessed spaces between Inter's midfield and defensive lines, while their wing-backs and Davis provided routes forward. Inter's circulation was comparatively measured.

### Adjustment

Inter increased the speed of circulation and the aggression of their reaction immediately after losing possession. Within the existing 3-5-2, Carlos Augusto operated higher, Diouf became more aggressive in depth, Barella moved into advanced zones and Thuram varied his positioning.

### Opponent response

Udinese's midfielders and defenders were increasingly forced to defend multiple heights and widths simultaneously. Their initial ability to progress declined as Inter pinned them deeper.

### Effect

Inter moved from vulnerability to Udinese's transitions to controlling the match territorially. Later personnel changes reinforced that control without requiring a fundamental formation change.

## Key Player: Carlos Augusto

Carlos Augusto's importance extended beyond his goal. His high positioning changed the geometry of Inter's attack.

By remaining high and wide, he forced Udinese to defend a larger horizontal space. That created conditions for Inter's central players to rotate and attack different vertical zones.

The key tactical point is that Carlos Augusto functioned as both a wing-back and a positional reference. His location influenced where Udinese could safely defend.

## Match Moment: Inter's 3–2

The third goal captured the tactical change clearly.

Early in the second half, Inter were already pinning Udinese back. A long ball sent Diouf into space on the right; he reached the byline and cut the ball back for Thuram, who finished into the far corner.

The sequence contained several elements that had become more pronounced since the opening phase: rapid progression after winning territory, a wing-back attacking depth, width creating access to the byline, Thuram arriving in a dangerous finishing position, and an attack that did not depend on prolonged central circulation.

## What Could Have Changed?

Udinese's strongest period came when they could play forward quickly through Davis and find Ekkelenkamp or Gómez between Inter's midfield and defensive lines.

A credible alternative would therefore have required Udinese to preserve those connections for longer once Inter increased their pressure.

However, the available evidence does not establish a single tactical adjustment that would certainly have changed the result. Inter's increased territorial control meant Udinese had fewer opportunities to launch the same types of forward attacks that had produced the first two goals.

Runjaić's later substitutions introduced fresh attacking and midfield personnel, but by that point Inter had established a substantial territorial advantage.

## The Larger Tactical Lesson

The match demonstrates why formation labels alone are insufficient to explain a tactical change.

Inter played 3-5-2 from the opening whistle to the final stages, yet the same formation produced two very different performances.

Early on, the structure was comparatively slow and vulnerable when Udinese attacked quickly. Later, the structure became fluid: one wing-back could stay high, the opposite wing-back could attack depth, a centre-back could advance into construction, Barella could move beyond the midfield line, Thuram could drop and reconnect play, and the team could react aggressively after losing possession.

The formation did not change. The relationships inside it did.

## Conclusion

Inter's 5–3 victory was not simply a story of recovering from a disastrous start. The more significant tactical development was the transformation of the team's existing 3-5-2.

Udinese initially exploited a slower Inter by finding wide areas and spaces between the lines. Inter responded without abandoning their basic structure. Instead, they increased the speed of possession, improved their occupation of the width, varied the heights of their midfielders and forwards, and became more aggressive immediately after losing the ball.

That combination gradually removed the conditions that had made Udinese dangerous.

By the second half, Inter were no longer playing merely to recover the score. They were controlling where the match was being played.

The three goals conceded still expose a defensive issue — particularly against rapid attacks and moments of transition — but the comeback showed that Chivu's team can alter the rhythm and positional relationships of a game without necessarily changing its nominal formation.

**Inter did not solve the match by changing the shape. They solved it by making the shape move faster.**

## Key Takeaways

1. **Inter's comeback began through a change in tempo, not a formation change.**
2. **Carlos Augusto's high positioning stretched Udinese horizontally and created central space for other players.**
3. **Thuram's movement away from the defensive line helped create lanes for Barella's forward runs.**
4. **Diouf's depth and Inter's wider positioning gave the 3-5-2 greater attacking variability.**
5. **More aggressive post-loss pressure reduced Udinese's ability to transition into the spaces that had hurt Inter early.**
6. **The 5–3 also showed that Inter's defensive transition management remained less stable than their attacking structure.**

## TGO Analyst View

* Both teams used three-centre-back structures, creating a tactical contest around width, midfield occupation and the spaces behind/around the opposing midfield.
* Udinese's 3-4-2-1 used Ekkelenkamp and Gómez behind Davis, while Inter's 3-5-2 supplied an additional central midfielder and two forwards.
* Inter's improvement was associated with faster circulation, more aggressive post-loss pressure, higher positioning from Carlos Augusto and increased forward movement from Diouf.
* Barella and Thuram developed an important positional relationship, with Thuram varying his depth and Barella making more advanced movements.
* Inter's attacking structure became more fluid without requiring a nominal formation change.
* Udinese's early attacking success came from direct progression, Davis' involvement and access to between-the-lines areas. Inter's own pre-match opposition analysis had identified these as established characteristics of Runjaić's team.

The match may contain a useful example of how a team can establish an effective early game plan but struggle to maintain it once the opponent changes its defensive/possession behaviour. The important question is **what changed**, not merely that Inter came back.
