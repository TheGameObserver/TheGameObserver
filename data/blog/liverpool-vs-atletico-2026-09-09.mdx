Liverpool's 2–1 comeback against Atlético Madrid was defined not simply by intensity, but by Andoni Iraola's ability to correct an initial pressing mismatch and turn a disjointed first phase into a more coordinated second-half performance.

## Executive Summary

Liverpool began their Champions League campaign with a 2–1 victory over Atlético Madrid at Anfield, but the result was shaped by a significant tactical adjustment. Atlético's unexpected back-three structure initially disrupted Liverpool's pressing references, allowing the visitors to create the opening goal and several dangerous moments.

Iraola acknowledged that Liverpool took time to match the structure correctly. After the adjustment, Liverpool's pressing became more coordinated, their midfielders could attack the box more effectively, and their attacking transitions became increasingly dangerous. Dominik Szoboszlai and Alexis Mac Allister supplied the goals, but the deeper tactical story was Liverpool's ability to correct their defensive organisation during the match and then impose greater pressure after half-time.

## Match Context

Liverpool opened their 2026/27 Champions League campaign with a 2–1 comeback victory over Atlético Madrid at Anfield.

Marcos Llorente put Atlético ahead in the 17th minute after Julián Álvarez played him through. Liverpool equalised through Dominik Szoboszlai in the 40th minute, before Alexis Mac Allister completed the comeback five minutes after half-time.

The match also marked Andoni Iraola's first Champions League match as Liverpool head coach.

## Tactical Setup

Liverpool started from a nominal 4-2-3-1:

Alisson; Ronald Araújo, Jérémy Jacquet, Virgil van Dijk, Milos Kerkez; Dominik Szoboszlai, Alexis Mac Allister; Bradley Barcola, Florian Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha; Alexander Isak.

Atlético's starting structure was different from the shape Liverpool had expected.

The visitors used a back-three system built around Dávid Hancko, Cristian Romero and Marc Pubill, with Marcos Llorente and Giuliano Simeone providing width and Koke and Pablo Barrios occupying central midfield roles. Julián Álvarez and Kang-in Lee operated as the advanced pair. The match was subsequently described by Iraola as an Atlético structure they had not used previously that season.

That distinction was tactically important.

Liverpool's initial pressing references were prepared for a different Atlético structure. The back three gave Atlético additional options in the first line and altered the relationships Liverpool needed to establish when pressing from their 4-2-3-1.

Iraola later accepted responsibility for Liverpool's slow start, explaining that Atlético's unexpected structure had made it difficult for Liverpool to match up correctly.

## Key Tactical Battle

### Liverpool had the intensity — but initially lacked the correct pressing references

#### Problem

Liverpool began aggressively.

The first action of the match almost produced a goal when Szoboszlai won possession immediately after kick-off and released Alexander Isak, whose effort went wide. Barcola then tested Jan Oblak after controlling a Szoboszlai pass.

The issue was therefore not a lack of aggression.

It was how that aggression was organised.

Atlético's back-three structure changed the number of players Liverpool had to account for in the first line of build-up. Liverpool's midfield and attacking players could not simply apply the same pressing references they had prepared for.

The structure appears to have disrupted Liverpool's initial pressing relationships: one Liverpool player could close the ball carrier while another Atlético centre-back remained available.

That created the possibility of Atlético bypassing the first pressure and finding the next line.

The result was a key distinction:

Liverpool were pressing, but the press was not initially synchronised.

That is different from simply saying Liverpool were being passive or tactically poor.

#### Structure

Liverpool's 4-2-3-1 gave them two central midfielders behind Wirtz, with Barcola and Ngumoha positioned to provide width and Isak leading the press.

Against Atlético's back three, however, the first line of pressure could become disconnected.

One Liverpool player could close the ball carrier while another Atlético centre-back remained available.

That created the possibility of Atlético bypassing the first pressure and finding the next line.

#### Movement and Behaviour

Atlético exploited the uncertainty by using their extra defender to circulate the ball and create additional passing options.

Julián Álvarez also provided a significant reference point between Liverpool's defensive and midfield lines.

The opening goal illustrated the danger.

Álvarez received the opportunity to play forward, and his pass released Llorente behind the Liverpool defensive line. Llorente finished over Alisson to make it 1–0.

The goal cannot by itself prove that Liverpool's entire defensive structure failed.

But it did demonstrate the consequence of Atlético finding a route through Liverpool's initial organisation.

#### Opponent Response

Liverpool did not abandon the original principles.

Instead, they corrected the relationships.

Iraola said that after Atlético's goal Liverpool took a few minutes to adjust and then began matching the structure better. He subsequently judged Liverpool to have been the better team in the second half.

That adjustment became the decisive tactical development.

## Tactical Evidence

### 1. Liverpool's pressing became more collective

The strongest evidence for the tactical adjustment comes from the contrast between the two halves.

Iraola explicitly stated that Liverpool's structure became better set after the initial adjustment. This matters because pressing effectiveness is not simply a question of how quickly individual players run toward the ball.

A press becomes more effective when:

- the first presser has cover;
- passing lanes are blocked;
- the next receiver is anticipated;
- the defensive line can step behind the pressure;
- the team moves as a connected unit.

Liverpool increasingly achieved those conditions after the adjustment.

### 2. Atlético's lead did not produce sustained attacking control

Atlético's most damaging moment came before Liverpool had fully corrected the structural mismatch.

Once Liverpool adjusted, Atlético struggled to maintain the same level of attacking threat.

Liverpool did not need to dominate possession to become the more dangerous side. Their superiority was better reflected in their chance production and attacking threat than in simple possession share.

### 3. Liverpool's midfield began contributing directly to the attack

The comeback was also evidence of a second Liverpool principle: midfielders were expected not merely to circulate possession but to arrive in the penalty area.

Iraola specifically highlighted the attacking value of Szoboszlai and Mac Allister's ability to arrive in the box.

That behaviour directly produced both Liverpool goals.

For the equaliser, Ronald Araújo flicked the ball inside the penalty area and Szoboszlai finished beyond Oblak.

For the winner, Alexander Isak was involved in the attacking sequence before Mac Allister received the ball around 20 yards from goal and struck into the corner.

The significance is structural.

Liverpool's midfield did not remain detached from the front line.

The midfielders were part of the final attacking line when the situation allowed it.

## Important Adjustments

### Initial Situation → Atlético disrupted Liverpool's references

Atlético's unexpected back-three structure created a problem for Liverpool's initial pressing organisation.

Liverpool could apply pressure but did not immediately have the correct relationships against the extra defender.

Atlético found enough space to progress and eventually produced the opening goal.

### Adjustment → Liverpool corrected the match-up

Liverpool adjusted their defensive organisation during the first half.

Iraola later confirmed that the team began matching Atlético's structure more effectively after conceding.

This was not a complete tactical reinvention.

The underlying principles remained aggressive pressure and forward defensive intent.

The change was in coordination.

### Opponent Response → Atlético struggled to sustain the initial advantage

Once Liverpool's press became more connected, Atlético found fewer opportunities to progress with the same consistency.

Liverpool also began generating more dangerous transition situations.

Barcola and Wirtz combined on a rapid counter immediately after half-time, with Barcola narrowly missing the opportunity to score.

Moments later, Liverpool scored the decisive goal.

## The Second-Half Turning Point

### Liverpool's pressure and attacking momentum converged

The period immediately after half-time was Liverpool's strongest phase.

Barcola's counter-attacking opportunity demonstrated the space available when Atlético's structure was stretched.

Then Ngumoha attacked the channel and delivered into the area. The ball ricocheted into the path of Mac Allister, who struck from around 20 yards to put Liverpool ahead.

The goal itself contained an element of deflection and therefore should not be presented as a deliberately constructed passing sequence from start to finish.

Liverpool had moved from struggling to match Atlético's structure to controlling the momentum of the game.

## Key Player: Dominik Szoboszlai

Szoboszlai's importance extended beyond his equalising goal.

His role connected Liverpool's midfield pressure with their attacking movements.

He was involved immediately in the opening seconds when he won possession and released Isak, and he later arrived inside the penalty area to score the equaliser.

Liverpool needed midfielders capable of moving forward once the structure had been stabilised.

Szoboszlai provided that connection.

## Match Moment

### 40' — Szoboszlai turns Liverpool's pressure into the equaliser

Liverpool's equaliser changed the tactical context of the match.

Ronald Araújo produced the decisive flick inside the box, allowing Szoboszlai to move onto the ball and finish.

The goal arrived shortly before half-time, but its significance extended beyond the scoreline.

Liverpool had survived Atlético's strongest period and entered the interval level.

That gave Iraola the opportunity to correct the structural issues that had affected the opening phase without having to chase the game from behind.

Five minutes after the restart, Liverpool completed the turnaround.

## What Could Have Changed?

Atlético's most credible alternative was to preserve the structural uncertainty that had troubled Liverpool during the opening phase.

The visitors were most dangerous when Liverpool's pressing references were unclear and Álvarez could receive or connect play before the defensive line was fully protected.

Once Liverpool corrected those relationships, Atlético struggled to reproduce the same threat.

However, the available evidence does not establish that a specific alternative formation or substitution would definitely have changed the result.

The defensible conclusion is narrower:

Atlético's strongest route was to continue exploiting the spaces created by Liverpool's initial pressing mismatch before Liverpool could stabilise the structure.

Once that opportunity disappeared, Liverpool became increasingly comfortable.

## Late-Game Management

Liverpool's victory was not entirely based on continued attacking pressure.

After Barcola was forced off, Iraola introduced Jeremie Frimpong and Víctor Muñoz to the flanks. Ryan Gravenberch replaced Mac Allister, while Kostas Tsimikas and Lewis Koumas came on late.

The changes gradually altered Liverpool's attacking profile.

The team still produced chances — Isak had an effort blocked by Robin Le Normand, while Frimpong had a late goal ruled out for offside — but Liverpool also had to manage Atlético's late attempts to recover.

That final phase is important because it shows the limits of describing the match simply as an uninterrupted Liverpool attacking performance.

Liverpool improved considerably after their initial adjustment, but Atlético remained capable of creating moments once Liverpool's attacking personnel changed.

## Conclusion

Liverpool's 2–1 victory was ultimately a test of tactical correction.

Atlético's unexpected back-three structure disrupted Liverpool's initial pressing references and contributed to a difficult opening phase. The visitors took advantage by finding Álvarez in a position to release Llorente, who opened the scoring.

Liverpool did not respond by abandoning their aggressive identity.

They adjusted the relationships within it.

Once the pressing structure became more coordinated, Liverpool began controlling more of the game's attacking momentum. Their midfielders were also able to make more aggressive forward movements, with Szoboszlai and Mac Allister both contributing goals.

The most important lesson from the match is therefore not simply that Liverpool showed character after conceding.

It is that Iraola identified a structural problem during the game, corrected Liverpool's pressing organisation, and subsequently saw his team's underlying attacking and defensive principles function with much greater clarity.

The win was not evidence of a perfect performance.

It was evidence of a team capable of correcting itself without abandoning its identity.

4. Liverpool's strongest evidence of development was not the comeback itself, but the ability to identify and correct a tactical problem during the match.
