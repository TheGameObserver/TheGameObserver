**Three first-half goals put PSG in control. City’s half-time changes improved their attack, but Akliouche’s finish kept the comeback out of reach.**

## Executive Summary

Paris Saint-Germain beat Manchester City 4–2 after building a 3–0 half-time lead. Désiré Doué, Ousmane Dembélé and Vitinha punished City during a decisive first-half spell.

City responded after the break. Iliman Ndiaye helped create Erling Haaland’s first goal, and Haaland scored again later. But Maghnes Akliouche’s goal between those finishes restored PSG’s three-goal advantage and blunted City’s momentum.

The contrast was clear: City improved their attacking threat, but could not erase the damage done before half-time.

## How PSG Took Control

City began with attacking intent, but PSG turned quick attacking sequences into goals. Doué opened the scoring on a break and then supplied Dembélé for the second. Vitinha added a third before half-time.

The speed of the first two goals left City little time to recover or adjust. PSG did not need to control every phase of the match; they needed to make their attacking moments count.

City’s challenge was balancing pressure on the ball with protection behind their advancing players. PSG’s early goals make that balance a credible area for investigation, although the available match account alone cannot prove that one pressing flaw caused all three goals.

## City Changed the Attack — But Not the Result

City made three substitutions at half-time, introducing Ndiaye, Matheus Nunes and Ayyoub Bouaddi. Their response became more dangerous, and Ndiaye crossed for Haaland to make it 3–1.

But Akliouche scored from outside the box in the 62nd minute, restoring PSG’s three-goal lead. Haaland’s second goal later reduced the margin again, but City could not complete the comeback.

The substitutions helped City create more threat; they did not guarantee control of the match. PSG’s fourth goal was decisive because it interrupted City’s recovery just as the game was becoming more competitive.

## Key Player: Iliman Ndiaye

Ndiaye made a direct contribution after coming on at half-time. He forced a save and then delivered the cross for Haaland’s first goal.

His impact showed how changed personnel could give City a new attacking option. It was a meaningful contribution, though not the sole explanation for City’s stronger second half.

## Conclusion

PSG’s victory was shaped by their ability to turn quick attacks into first-half goals. City improved after the break, but Akliouche’s finish repeatedly pushed the comeback beyond reach.

**City found more attacking solutions after half-time; PSG had already made their early chances count.**

## Key Takeaways

- PSG’s quick attacks produced three first-half goals.
- City’s half-time substitutions increased their attacking threat.
- Ndiaye contributed directly to Haaland’s first goal.
- Akliouche’s goal interrupted City’s comeback.
- City’s pressing balance merits further video analysis, but the exact causes of PSG’s goals require closer evidence.

## TGO ANALYST VIEW

The key question is not simply why City conceded three times before half-time, but **how PSG progressed from City’s pressure into scoring situations before the defence could recover**. A focused video review should track the distance between City’s pressing line, midfield and back line during the first three goals, then compare those relationships with City’s second-half response.

That would help distinguish whether PSG repeatedly exploited the same structural space or whether different attacking situations produced the goals. Until that review is completed, pressing imbalance is a strong investigative lead—not a proven single cause.
