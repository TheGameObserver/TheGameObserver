# Executive Summary

Manchester City's 1–0 victory over Manchester United was transformed by Phil Foden's 23rd-minute red card. The decisive tactical story, however, was City's response.

Rather than preserving their original 4-2-3-1 with ten players, Enzo Maresca shifted City into a compact 4-4-1. The priority became protecting central spaces, denying runs in behind and retaining an attacking outlet for transitions.

United gained possession, territory and shot volume, but struggled to turn their numerical advantage into consistent central penetration.

**United had the extra player. City controlled the spaces.**

# Match Context

Manchester City defeated Manchester United 1–0 at Old Trafford on Sunday, 13 September 2026.

Both teams began with nominal 4-2-3-1 structures. United used Senne Lammens in goal, with Diogo Dalot, Harry Maguire, Lisandro Martínez and Patrick Dorgu in defence; Youri Tielemans and Kobbie Mainoo in midfield; and Bryan Mbeumo, Bruno Fernandes and Marcus Rashford behind Matheus Cunha.

City started with Gianluigi Donnarumma, Matheus Nunes, Rúben Dias, Marc Guéhi and Joško Gvardiol; Elliot Anderson and Enzo Fernández; Phil Foden, Rayan Cherki and Antoine Semenyo; and Erling Haaland.

The defining event came in the 23rd minute when Foden was sent off for violent conduct following his clash with Bruno Fernandes. VAR confirmed the red card.

Haaland eventually scored in the 60th minute, with VAR overturning the assistant referee's initial offside decision.

# Tactical Setup

## Manchester United

United started from a **4-2-3-1**, but Matheus Cunha operated as a false nine.

His movement was intended to connect United's midfield and attacking players while creating space for Marcus Rashford and Bryan Mbeumo to attack from wider positions.

Bruno Fernandes occupied the central attacking-midfield role, with Kobbie Mainoo and Youri Tielemans providing the midfield base.

With Cunha moving away from the central defensive line, United did not always have a fixed reference occupying City's centre-backs. That became increasingly relevant once City dropped into a compact block.

## Manchester City

City's initial structure was also a 4-2-3-1. Elliot Anderson and Enzo Fernández formed the midfield base, with Foden, Cherki and Semenyo supporting Haaland.

Before the red card, City attempted to create midfield overloads and maintain an aggressive attacking structure. After Foden's dismissal, that plan was no longer sustainable.

City changed to a **4-4-1**.

The objective was to protect the centre, deny United space behind the defensive line and attack when possession was recovered.

# Key Tactical Battle

## City's numerical disadvantage removed United's best attacking spaces

Foden's red card appeared to give United the decisive structural advantage. They had more than an hour against ten players at Old Trafford.

But City's response changed the problem.

Rather than attempting to preserve their normal 4-2-3-1 relationships with one player missing, City simplified the structure and formed a compact 4-4-1.

The change reduced the spaces between their defensive and midfield lines and made it more difficult for United to access the centre.

Four midfielders could protect the width of the pitch while the remaining forward stayed available as an outlet. The back four remained relatively close to the midfield line, reducing the space United could attack centrally.

City therefore conceded territory without necessarily conceding the spaces that mattered most.

Maresca explained after the match that, once City went down to ten, preventing transitions and space in behind became a priority.

## United had possession, but not enough penetration

United circulated the ball more frequently after the red card, but City's compactness meant that much of the possession occurred outside the most valuable central zones.

United could move the ball from full-back to winger and back again. They could cross. They could recycle.

But repeatedly entering the final third is not the same as repeatedly breaking the defensive line.

United's numerical advantage created territorial pressure, but it did not consistently translate into high-quality central chances.

That distinction became the central tactical story of the match.

# Tactical Evidence

## City changed shape immediately after the red card

Foden's dismissal forced City to abandon their original attacking structure and move into a 4-4-1.

The adjustment was not simply a deeper defensive position. It changed City's priorities:

**one player fewer → greater compactness → protect central spaces → counter when possible.**

City's reduced attacking presence was the price of protecting the areas United wanted to exploit.

## City retained an attacking outlet

After going down to ten, City did not become completely passive.

Maresca replaced Rayan Cherki with Iliman Ndiaye at half-time, preserving pace and an outlet for moments when United committed numbers forward.

City could not afford prolonged possession battles against an eleven-man United side. But they could use Haaland, Semenyo and Ndiaye as outlets when possession was recovered.

That meant United still had to retain some defensive responsibility rather than committing every player forward.

# Important Adjustments

## Initial Situation — City attack with 11

City's 4-2-3-1 used Anderson and Fernández as the midfield base, with Foden, Cherki and Semenyo supporting Haaland.

The opening period was relatively even, with City creating early opportunities through Semenyo and Enzo Fernández.

## Adjustment — Foden sent off

The red card fundamentally altered the tactical problem.

City moved into a 4-4-1, prioritising defensive compactness, central protection and preventing space in behind.

## United Response — More possession and numbers

United pushed higher, with their full-backs and wide players becoming increasingly involved while Fernandes continued to operate between the lines.

The pressure became particularly strong after half-time, when Mainoo struck the post following Rashford's first-half effort against the same upright.

## City Response — Preserve an outlet

Maresca replaced Cherki with Ndiaye at half-time rather than simply adding another defensive player.

The change preserved an outlet capable of carrying the ball forward when City recovered possession.

## Effect — One decisive transition

The game remained 0–0 until the 60th minute.

City then broke forward. Gvardiol advanced from the left and delivered toward Haaland. The cross was deflected, but Haaland attacked the resulting ball and finished from a tight angle.

The goal was initially ruled out for offside, but VAR determined that Haaland was onside and that Enzo Fernández, although in an offside position, had not played the ball or interfered with the play.

City had turned a defensive game state into the winning attack.

# Key Player: Enzo Fernández

Enzo Fernández was arguably City's most tactically significant player.

His role changed after Foden's dismissal. From the midfield base, he now had to contribute to City's compact defensive structure while remaining capable of supporting the transition when possession was recovered.

He was also involved in the winning sequence. Although he was in an offside position when Gvardiol's cross entered the box, VAR determined that Fernández had neither played the ball nor interfered with the defender.

His ability to cover wider spaces had also been part of City's initial midfield structure, giving Maresca flexibility before the red card.

# What Could Have Changed?

United's clearest tactical alternative was to create greater central occupation against City's 4-4-1.

Once City reduced their defensive spacing, simply circulating the ball around the outside became increasingly predictable.

United could have increased their central occupation earlier, using a more fixed striker to occupy City's centre-backs while Fernandes and the wide players attacked around the block.

The eventual introduction of Benjamin Šeško moved in that direction, but it came at 67 minutes, after City had already scored.

However, there is no evidence that an earlier change would definitely have produced a goal. United did create genuine opportunities, with Rashford and Mainoo hitting the woodwork and Mbeumo forcing a late save from Donnarumma.

The stronger conclusion is that United needed **more central occupation and combination play**, not simply more players attacking the box.

# The VAR Context

The winning goal remains relevant because it determined the final game state.

The assistant referee initially flagged Haaland for offside. VAR overturned the decision, determining that Haaland was onside and that Fernández, despite being in an offside position, had not interfered with play.

The decision was subsequently criticized by United, and The Guardian reported that Pro Ref later contacted the club to acknowledge an "error of judgment" in allowing the goal to stand.

The goal was nevertheless awarded under the final official decision.

For the tactical story, the important point is that City found an attacking route despite being a player down, while United failed to prevent the delivery into Haaland's finishing zone.

# Wider Implications

For Manchester City, the victory provides an early indication of Maresca's ability to manage radically different game states.

The original plan was based on attacking with eleven. After Foden's dismissal, City needed a different footballing logic: defend more compactly, surrender possession, maintain an attacking outlet and wait for moments when United's structure became vulnerable.

That adaptability is particularly important because City began the season with four consecutive Premier League victories, remaining on a maximum 12 points after four matches.

For Manchester United, the match exposes a different issue.

The problem was not a lack of territorial access. It was converting territorial access into enough central threat.

Against opponents who deliberately surrender possession and protect the space behind their midfield line, United will need more mechanisms for breaking compact defensive structures.

That challenge becomes particularly important if Cunha continues to operate as a false nine.

# Conclusion

Manchester City's 1–0 victory was ultimately a story of structural adaptation.

Foden's red card removed a player from City's attacking structure, but Maresca's response gave the remaining ten players a clearer defensive task: protect the centre, deny space in behind and preserve an outlet for transitions.

United controlled possession and territory but never consistently converted that superiority into central penetration. City, meanwhile, accepted the loss of the ball in exchange for control of the spaces that mattered most.

Haaland's winner was the payoff.

**An extra player only becomes a decisive advantage when a team can create usable space with it. United had the numerical superiority; City controlled where the game could be played.**

# Key Takeaways

1. **Foden's 23rd-minute red card completely changed City's tactical priorities.**
2. **Maresca's shift into a compact 4-4-1 denied United the spaces needed to turn numerical superiority into sustained central threat.**
3. **United produced territorial pressure and shot volume but struggled to create consistent central penetration.**
4. **City retained an attacking outlet rather than becoming completely passive, allowing them to threaten despite being down to ten.**
5. **Haaland's winner illustrated City's post-red-card model: compact defending, selective progression and decisive finishing.**
