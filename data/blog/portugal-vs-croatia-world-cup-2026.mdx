# Portugal's Control Was Real — But Croatia Found the Space Behind It

Portugal controlled the first half through aggressive pressure and territorial possession, but Croatia's second-half adjustment exposed the space behind that pressure before Portugal's structural changes and wide delivery mechanism decided the tie.



## Executive Summary



Portugal's 2–1 victory over Croatia was shaped by two contrasting tactical phases. Roberto Martínez's initial structure gave Portugal territorial control and repeatedly disrupted Croatia's build-up, but the aggressive positioning of João Neves and Vitinha also left space behind the first line of pressure. Croatia exploited that space after half-time by playing more directly and creating repeated situations against Portugal's back four.



Martínez responded with a four-player substitution and subsequently changed the midfield balance. Portugal regained enough control to survive Croatia's improved attacking threat, while their established use of wide deliveries ultimately produced the winning goal: Rafael Leão's cross found substitute Gonçalo Ramos in stoppage time. FIFA's Technical Study Group later identified this wide-crossing mechanism as a recurring feature of Portugal's attacking strategy throughout the match.



---



## Match Context



Portugal defeated Croatia **2–1** in the FIFA World Cup Round of 32 at Toronto Stadium.



Portugal progressed to the Round of 16.



Ivan Perišić gave Croatia the lead in the 53rd minute. Cristiano Ronaldo equalised from the penalty spot in the 68th, before Gonçalo Ramos headed Portugal's winner in the fourth minute of added time.



Portugal finished with **61% possession, 15 shots and nine corners**, while Croatia had 40% possession, 13 shots and five corners. The most revealing contrast, however, was not possession itself: Croatia managed **six shots on target to Portugal's three**, demonstrating that their second-half threat was genuine despite Portugal's territorial advantage.



---



## Tactical Setup



Both teams started from a nominal **4-2-3-1**. Portugal's starting XI featured Diogo Costa; João Cancelo, Rúben Dias, Renato Veiga, Nuno Mendes; João Neves, Vitinha; Pedro Neto, Bruno Fernandes, Rafael Leão; and Cristiano Ronaldo.



Croatia started with Dominik Livaković; Josip Stanišić, Marin Pongračić, Josip Šutalo, Mateo Kovačić, Luka Modrić, Ante Budimir, Nikola Vlašić, Ivan Perišić, Martin Baturina and Petar Sučić.



The nominal formations, however, do not fully describe Portugal's defensive behaviour.



Portugal used an aggressive man-oriented pressing approach. João Neves and Vitinha frequently stepped forward from the double pivot, attempting to disrupt Croatia before the visitors could establish controlled possession. According to Squawka's Opta-based analysis, this pressure was a major reason Portugal controlled the opening period.



In possession, Portugal also sought to create wide delivery opportunities through deeper midfield positioning, rotations and switches. FIFA's Technical Study Group subsequently confirmed that Portugal deliberately used deep midfielders alongside the centre-backs to create build-up overloads before moving the ball quickly toward the opposite flank.

## Key Tactical Battle



### Portugal's press controlled Croatia — until Croatia stopped trying to play through it



#### Problems



Croatia initially struggled to progress consistently against Portugal's aggressive pressure.



Portugal's midfielders were not content to protect the back four. Neves and Vitinha regularly stepped forward, compressing the available central space and forcing Croatia into uncomfortable decisions.



That helped Portugal establish territorial control.



But the same structure contained a potential weakness.



When both midfielders advanced aggressively, the protection immediately in front of Portugal's centre-backs became thinner.



#### Structure



Portugal's pressing structure effectively asked the midfield to defend forward.



That produced a useful first-half trade-off:



**Higher midfield pressure**



→ reduced Croatian central progression



→ more Portuguese territorial possession



→ fewer Croatian sustained attacks.



But the opposite consequence was also possible:



**Croatia bypasses first pressure**



→ midfield line is behind the ball



→ Portugal's back four becomes exposed



→ Croatian attackers can attack the defensive line directly.



That second scenario became increasingly important after half-time.



#### Movement and Behaviour



Croatia changed its attacking behaviour.



Rather than repeatedly attempting to progress through Portugal's pressure, Croatia became more willing to play longer and more directly into advanced areas.



Croatia repeatedly created **4v4 situations** against Portugal's defensive line after the change in approach.



This was the critical tactical adjustment of the match.



Croatia had discovered that Portugal's aggressive press was not only something to escape — **it could be attacked**.



#### Opponent Response



The first-half Portuguese advantage therefore became less decisive after the interval.



Croatia began generating situations in which their wide players and overlapping full-backs could attack Portugal's defensive structure.



The clearest example arrived for the opening goal.



---



### Perišić's Goal: When Portugal's Pressure Became a Liability



At 53 minutes, Croatia attacked down the right.



Josip Stanišić advanced and delivered into the penalty area, while Ivan Perišić attacked the far side.



Squawka's sequence analysis highlights the positional problem: Nuno Mendes was drawn toward the ball, while Croatian attackers occupied the central defenders. Perišić was consequently left in a favourable position at the far post.



The important point is that Croatia did not create the goal simply through numerical superiority.



Squawka explicitly describes the situation as **three Portuguese defenders against three Croatian forwards**, but with Croatia obtaining a positional advantage inside the box.



This distinction matters.



The problem was not necessarily that Portugal had "too few defenders". It was that the defenders were **poorly connected relative to the attacking positions**.



Croatia had manipulated the relationships inside the box sufficiently to give Perišić the decisive advantage.



---



## Portugal's Second Tactical Mechanism: Creating Crossing Conditions



Portugal's attacking strategy was more deliberate than simply putting crosses into the box.



FIFA's Technical Study Group identified **15 open-play crosses**, with 14 originating from wide channels — seven from each side. Two were successful, including Ramos' winning header.



The mechanism was:



**Deep midfield positioning**



→ Croatia drawn toward one side



→ quick switch



→ opposite-side numerical advantage



→ wide player receives with time



→ inswinging delivery



→ central/far-post attack.



FIFA specifically identifies this as an intentional build-up strategy rather than incidental crossing.



This gives the match an important tactical symmetry.



Croatia's goal came from exploiting Portugal's vulnerability to wide progression and far-post occupation.



Portugal's winning goal came from exploiting Croatia in a similar area.



---



## Key Player: Rafael Leão



Leão's importance was tactical rather than simply statistical.



He provided Portugal with a way to attack Croatia's defensive line without requiring Bruno Fernandes to consistently find central space.



When Croatia's midfield compacted the centre, Leão offered:



- width;

- depth;

- one-versus-one threat;

- an outlet after switches;

- crossing quality from the left.



His final contribution was decisive: he delivered the cross that Ramos converted in stoppage time.



Leão therefore represented the continuation of Portugal's attacking mechanism through a match in which the central spaces were difficult to dominate consistently.



---



## Match Moment



### 90+4' — The same pattern, executed decisively



Portugal's winner was not a random late cross.



Rafael Leão supplied an inswinging delivery from the left.



Gonçalo Ramos attacked the target area and headed the ball beyond Livaković.



FIFA's Technical Study Group specifically uses this goal as the clearest example of Portugal's recurring crossing strategy. The organisation notes that Portugal repeatedly slowed play, created overloads, switched the ball and then attacked the goal area with speed.



The sequence therefore completes the tactical story of the match.



Croatia had exploited Portugal's defensive vulnerability through wide delivery.



Portugal eventually used the same broad attacking principle against Croatia.



---



## What Could Have Changed?



Croatia's second-half approach provides the clearest alternative route.



Rather than attempting to match Portugal's possession, Croatia found greater success when they:



1. bypassed the initial pressure;

2. played more directly;

3. created 4v4 situations;

4. attacked wide areas;

5. filled the far-post zone.



This was effective enough to produce the opening goal and several further dangerous situations. Squawka notes another sequence in which Croatia created a favourable far-post crossing opportunity for Igor Matanović after Portugal's pressure had again been bypassed.



The problem for Croatia was sustainability.



Portugal's territorial advantage meant Croatia had fewer opportunities to repeatedly execute this mechanism, while Martínez's midfield adjustment reduced some of the space available behind the first line of pressure.



Croatia also had a late equaliser ruled out for offside, meaning the final minutes remained extremely volatile.



---



## Tournament / Wider Implications



Portugal's victory exposed an important issue that became relevant to their next knockout test.



Their pressing structure could generate territorial dominance, but against opponents capable of consistently bypassing the first pressure, the protection behind the midfield line became critical.



The subsequent Round of 16 defeat to Spain means this match should not be interpreted as evidence of a fully resolved Portuguese structure. FBref records Portugal's World Cup campaign ending with a 0–1 defeat to Spain in the next round.



The broader lesson is therefore narrower:



**Portugal had enough attacking quality and tactical flexibility to overcome Croatia, but their aggressive pressure required careful midfield protection once opponents stopped trying to play through it.**



---



## Conclusion



Portugal's 2–1 victory was a match of **control without complete control**.



For the opening phase, Martínez's aggressive pressing structure worked. Portugal controlled possession and territory while restricting Croatia's ability to progress cleanly.



Croatia's response changed the game.



By playing more directly and attacking the spaces behind Portugal's advancing midfielders, they converted Portugal's main defensive strength — aggressive pressure — into a potential weakness.



Martínez responded by changing the midfield balance and introducing fresh attacking players. Portugal did not abandon their wide attacking mechanism, however. Their repeated use of switches and inswinging deliveries remained a consistent route toward the box.



That mechanism eventually decided the match.



Leão's cross to Ramos in stoppage time was not an isolated moment of fortune. It was the final and most valuable execution of a pattern Portugal had repeatedly attempted throughout the match.



The tactical story, therefore, is not simply **Portugal dominated and won 2–1**.



It is that **Portugal's initial pressure controlled Croatia until Croatia learned to bypass it; Martínez then restored structural balance while Portugal's wide attacking mechanism continued to function well enough to decide the match.**



---



## Key Takeaways



1. **Portugal's first-half dominance was primarily territorial and pressing-based, not complete chance control.**

2. **Croatia improved by bypassing Portugal's aggressive midfield pressure rather than attempting to outplay it through possession.**

3. **The Perišić goal exposed a positional problem in Portugal's defensive structure, particularly around wide progression and far-post occupation.**

4. **Martínez's substitutions addressed the balance between forward pressing and midfield protection.**

5. **Portugal's wide-switching and crossing strategy remained their most repeatable attacking mechanism, culminating in Ramos' winning header.**
